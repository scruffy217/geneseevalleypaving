H2: Why Asphalt Breaks Down Faster in Rochester

Rochester sits on the south shore of Lake Ontario, and that means lake-effect snow. More snow means more plowing, and plow blades chew up driveway edges and catch on raised spots.

Then there's salt. Salt melts the ice, but the water it leaves behind soaks into any crack it can find. At night it freezes, expands and pushes the crack wider. Do that all winter and you get the potholes everyone sees in March.

That's why we spend so much time on the part you never see. A firm base, the right slope and a good seal are what keep a Rochester driveway in one piece.

[IMAGE: Winter-damaged driveway edge next to a snowbank | Alt: Winter damage to an asphalt driveway in Rochester NY | File: rochester-winter-driveway-damage.webp]

H2: Getting Your Driveway Ready for a Rochester Winter

Fall is when you get ahead of it. Here's what we tell customers before the first plow comes through.

1. Fill cracks before the first freeze. Open cracks are where the water gets in.
2. Fix potholes and broken edges. Plows catch on them and make them worse.
3. Seal while it's still warm. Sealcoat needs warm, dry days, so early fall is the last good window.
4. Mark your edges. Driveway stakes show the plow where the asphalt ends and the lawn starts.
5. Talk to your plow guy. Ask him to lift the blade a little or use plow shoes, especially on new asphalt.

H2: Rochester Paving Questions We Hear a Lot

H3: When is paving season in Rochester?
Hot mix asphalt needs warm, dry weather to go down right. Most paving happens from spring through late fall. Once the cold settles in for good, hot mix cools too fast to pack down right, and the season ends until spring. The calendar fills up, so call early.

H3: Does road salt hurt my driveway?
Salt doesn't eat asphalt directly. The problem is the water it leaves behind. Salty meltwater runs into cracks, freezes at night and pushes them wider. It also wears down sealcoat faster. Keeping cracks filled and the surface sealed is the best defense.

H3: How do I protect a new driveway from snowplows?
Mark the edges with stakes before the first storm. Ask your plow service to lift the blade a little or use plow shoes the first winter. Try not to pile heavy snowbanks on the edges, since that's where new asphalt is softest.

H3: When is it too cold to sealcoat?
Sealcoat needs warm, dry days to cure. Most sealer makers call for at least 50 degrees and rising, with no rain coming. In Rochester, that usually means late spring through early fall.

H3: How long before I can drive on my new driveway?
Usually about three days for a car. Wait longer for heavy trucks, and longer in hot weather. Try not to turn your wheels while the car is sitting still. That scuffs soft asphalt.

H3: When should I sealcoat a new driveway?
New asphalt needs time to cure first. We usually suggest waiting six months to a year before the first seal.

H3: Do I need a full replacement or just an overlay?
It comes down to the base. If the base is solid and the cracks are on the surface, an overlay can work. If the driveway is sinking, heaving or breaking apart in chunks, the base has failed and it needs to come out.

H3: Do I need a permit to pave my driveway?
It depends on the work and where you live. Rules in the City of Rochester can be different from the rules in Greece, Pittsford or Webster. We check with the city or your town before the job starts. [CONFIRM contractor handles permits]

H3: Are recycled millings a good choice?
For long drives, farm roads and tight budgets, yes. They make a lot of sense in the rural parts of the county, like Riga, Rush and Wheatland. They cost less and get firmer over time. They won't look as smooth or finished as hot mix asphalt.

H3: Do you work in the city or just the suburbs?
Both. We pave downtown near High Falls and The Strong National Museum of Play, out to the lake in Charlotte, and in every town in Monroe County.

H3: Do you work on gravel driveways too?
Yes. New installs, regrading, and fixing ruts, washouts and potholes.
